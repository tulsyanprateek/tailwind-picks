How Tailwind Picks works

A rule-based way to own the strongest Nifty 100 stocks, only while the market is healthy. Every number on the dashboard comes from the rules below.

The philosophy

Follow the trend, don't predict

We don't guess what a company will earn. We buy what the market is already proving strong.

Winners keep winning, for a while

Stocks that rose the most over 3 to 12 months tend to keep beating the rest for a few months. It is one of the most researched patterns in markets.

Market first, stock second

Even great stocks fall in a falling market. We only buy when the whole market is rising.

Cut losers, let winners run

Every stock gets a 15% stop loss. Winners are kept as long as they stay in the Top 10.

Rules, not feelings

The same rules every time, tested on 12 years of prices. No tips, no news, no excitement.

Few decisions, low cost

Check twice a month only. Fewer trades means lower charges and less stress.

The technical indicators

Blue tags are the tested rules that decide what to buy and sell. Purple tags are popular indicators shown as a second opinion, so you can cross-check each stock on the dashboard (Indicators tab). They don't change the picks, because adding untested rules to a tested method makes it less reliable, not more.

USED IN THE RULES

Market filter (Nifty 50)

What it is: Whether the whole market is in an uptrend: the Nifty 50 above its 200-day average, and its 50-day average above the 200-day.

How to read it: When the market is weak, most stocks fall together, even good ones. So no new buys, and everything is sold at the next check-in.

USED IN THE RULES

Moving averages (50-day and 200-day)

What it is: The average closing price of the last 50 days (about 2.5 months) and the last 200 days (about 10 months).

How to read it: Price above both, 50-day above 200-day, and the 200-day rising = a healthy uptrend. These are the most watched trend lines in the world, so many buyers and sellers act around them.

USED IN THE RULES

52-week high and low

What it is: The highest and lowest prices of the last year.

How to read it: A strong stock trades close to its 1-year high (within 25%) and well above its low (at least 30% higher).

USED IN THE RULES

Momentum (relative strength)

What it is: How much a stock rose over 3, 6 and 12 months (40%, 30%, 30% weight), skipping the last week, compared with every other Nifty 100 stock.

How to read it: This is the main engine. Only the top 30% qualify, and the highest ranked are bought. The last week is skipped because very short-term moves tend to reverse.

USED IN THE RULES

ATR (average true range)

What it is: How much a stock typically moves in a day.

How to read it: Used only in the 12-year test to compare stop loss choices. The live stop loss is a simple 15% below the buy price.

SECOND OPINION

RSI (14)

What it is: Relative Strength Index: compares recent up days with down days, on a scale of 0 to 100.

How to read it: 50 to 70 = healthy momentum. Above 70 = very strong but stretched, may pause. Below 50 = momentum fading.

SECOND OPINION

MACD (12, 26, 9)

What it is: Moving Average Convergence Divergence: the gap between a fast and a slow average, with its own signal line.

How to read it: Above zero and above its signal line = momentum rising. Above zero but below the signal line = cooling. Below zero = downtrend.

SECOND OPINION

ADX (14)

What it is: Average Directional Index: how strong the trend is, with +DI and -DI showing whether buyers or sellers are in control.

How to read it: Above 25 with buyers in control = strong uptrend. Below 20 = no clear trend.

SECOND OPINION

Volume and OBV

What it is: On-Balance Volume adds volume on up days and subtracts it on down days.

How to read it: Rising OBV = buyers are stepping in and the move has support. Falling OBV = more selling than buying.

Why the 50-day and 200-day averages?

An average smooths out daily noise and shows the direction. 50 days (about 2.5 months) captures the current move; 200 days (about 10 months) captures the big trend. They are the trend lines most funds and traders watch, so prices often react around them, which makes them more reliable than unusual settings.

What the signals mean

BUY every rule passes and the stock is in the Top 5.

HOLD still healthy and in the Top 10: keep it if you own it, don't add.

SELL a rule failed or it dropped out of the Top 10: sell on the next check-in day.

WAIT the stock is strong but the market is weak: don't buy yet.

Why check every 15 days?

We tested different check-in intervals on 2014 to 2020 only, each with different calendar dates. The less often you check, the more the result depends on lucky or unlucky dates: checking every 3 months gave anything from a weak to a great return depending only on which months were chosen. Every 2 weeks gave steady results with fewer trades than weekly, so that is what the radar uses.

Buy price, stop loss and target

Buy up to: last close + 2%, as a limit order before 9:15 am. If it opens higher and never comes back, skip it.

Stop loss: 15% below your buy price, set once as a GTT order on Kite.

Target: +25%, the typical winning trade in the test. A guide only: winners are kept while they stay in the Top 10.

Sell everything when the market turns weak on a check-in day.
